## You Write For Humans

When you send someone a text, do you want them to read it? I mean, yes, right? You wrote it for them. They're the intended recipient. Of course you want them to read it.

There's some message, some meaning, some knowledge, some emotion that you mean to convey in your writing. This is obvious. You know this. I know this. We all know this.

But what happens when someone sends you something they didn't write? That wasn't even written by a human? At least [ghostwriters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostwriter), who've been a thing forever, used to be human. These days with AI, who knows?

After seeing how some people are [using AI to write personal messages](https://theconversation.com/most-people-do-not-realize-when-a-personal-message-they-receive-was-written-by-ai-study-finds-278874), from texts and emails to messages on TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, Facebook, WhatsApp, Teams, Slack, Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Discord, and who knows what else, I'm starting to wonder whether we're all on the same page about what I thought was common sense.

When you write, you want people to read it.

**You write for humans**.

So, if you didn't write it, why should I read it?  Why would I bother to read something you couldn't be bothered to write?

## Your Words Matter

We've [crossed the Rubicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Rubicon). People are sending other people AI generated messages, and on the receiving end, it's not always being noticed, because [LLMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) have gotten better.

[This 💩 is bananas, B-A-N-A-N-A-S](https://youtu.be/_jAAixDgHhM?si=TZmPiouFyKrsHq1h&t=142).

It's not a good thing. I don't like it, and I'm not alone.

There's a great article going viral right now in the tech community that calls out how [*not very cash money*](https://knowyourmeme.com/sensitive/memes/that-wasnt-very-cash-money-of-you) it is to pass off AI generated text as your own.

> [*Niklas Gruhn - Don't Be a Meat Proxy*](https://gruhn.me/blog/2026-08-03/)

It's a great short read. Here's another good one on a similar theme.

> [*Sophie Koonin - This Website Is For Humans*](https://localghost.dev/blog/this-website-is-for-humans/)

Their point and my point are largely the same. You write because you want people to read it. If you didn't write it, why should I read it? If it wasn't important enough for you to write, why should it be important enough for me to read?

## Bring Back Writing Letters

I find myself longing for the days when people wrote letters to one another. Bring back pen pals. I'm partial to a good rom-com, particularly from the 90s and early 2000s (less raunchy, more wholesome), and [*You've Got Mail*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27ve_Got_Mail) is one of my favorites.

While [Meg Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Ryan) and [Tom Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hanks) weren't writing physical letters, they were digital pen pals, with each of their **unique voices** shining through their emails. Which eventually led them to romance, despite their differences.

Can you imagine a version of that film where each side used ChatGPT to compose their emails? It wouldn't work! The love, the romance, the connection, it was found in the nuances of their literary voices. Buried in words **they wrote themselves**.

Now, to be fair, both characters asked people around them for feedback on their emails. Which today is like showing a text to a friend to make sure you don't sound too desperate or clingy, and then maybe accepting a suggestion or two. But does that make the text any less you? No! Having an editor is fine. Outsourcing the writing altogether, then putting your name on it, is not.

**An Aside:** If you haven't seen [*You've Got Mail*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You've_Got_Mail), go watch it. You'll be happy by the end. You just smile the whole time and wish real life worked like that. Then go watch [*Sleepless in Seattle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepless_in_Seattle) for another Meg Ryan + Tom Hanks pairing. You'll also be happy by the end, as long as you can suspend your disbelief a bit during the final act.

## For Whom Do We Toil?

What is the point of writing? Of communication? What sense does it make to send someone something you didn't write as though it came from you?

It's one thing in a formal setting like work or public speeches or even books from published authors with teams of editors, but for us everyday people...

[My brother in Christ](https://knowyourmeme.com/sensitive/memes/my-brother-in-christ), why are you sending me AI generated messages? Did **YOU** even read it? And even if you did, why did you generate it to begin with? You can't just text me? [Are you okay](https://www.tiktok.com/@solobobo29/video/7461690032101821728)? [Are you mad](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OCoQ-pMkXFg)? [You dey craze](http://naijalingo.com/words/u-dey-craze)?

[The nerve, the audacity, the unmitigated gall](https://youtu.be/Q0ib0nzJMGs?si=IKcnKZQPPkr7lALl&t=16), it takes for someone to outsource the hard work of writing, then expect someone else to do the cognitive work of reading, it's [blasphemous](https://youtu.be/1OEnzTDhpfE?si=0npKyCSrBKnQ1uBM&t=38)!

**An Aside:** The only *maybe* defensible excuse for doing this in direct messages is writing in a non-native language. In that case, yeah. I totally would just to make sure I didn't sound silly. But if you're speaking your native tongue, what are you doing?
{: .notice--info}

This all leads me to some more great articles.

> [*Tom Bedor - If You Are Asking For Human Attention, Demonstrate Human Effort*](https://tombedor.dev/human-attention-and-human-effort/)

[Bedor](https://tombedor.dev/about/) makes several great points with a specific focus on our shared profession of software engineering.

But for a more general take, there's this:

> [*Bret Stephens - I'm Begging You: Never Write With A.I.*](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/04/opinion/artificial-intelligence-ai-writing.html?unlocked_article_code=1.3VA.LFlM.iKEZ2eNnfO4m&smid=url-share)

That one is so very well written. [Bret Stephens](https://www.nytimes.com/by/bret-stephens) is firmly in the anti AI camp, which I'm not, but I mostly agree with him about AI's role in writing. He says it should have no role at all. I think its role, if it's to have one at all (you don't need it!), should be minimal, similar to tools like spellcheck or Grammarly.

## AI Transparency

Thankfully, some platforms are doing stuff to help address this problem. [Substack now lets readers check whether an article was AI generated](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/50891130623508-How-can-I-detect-AI-on-Substack), and [LinkedIn lets users report AI slop posts](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/linkedin-adds-a-button-to-report-ai-generated-slop/).
